## Working organizing direction — broadly endorsed at exchange 55

**The archive attempts to define them; the living characters exceed those definitions.** Sell thrilling people and a consequential mission, not colonial racial theory. Avoid making genuine period praise function as an authoritative explanation of inherited powers. Character individuality, choice, humor and mutual responsibility must become visible, not just three ethnic labels and signature weapons.

Suggested flexible sequence (no runtime or platform locked):

1. Open in motion: the rider/gallop/dust image, with physical sound and an immediate visual hook. Let the page enter after the audience has encountered a living person, rather than beginning with a lengthy history lecture.
2. Brief archival interruption: a real, attributed phrase or page fragment introduces how imperial authorities classified and valued soldiers. Graphic transitions can carry page texture/ink into the designed world. New text, fictional dossiers and recreated imagery must not masquerade as genuine archival evidence.
3. Distinct entrances: each hero gets a memorable action plus a small behavioral choice, not an inventory of culture, vehicle, weapon and power. Possible emphases: Pashtun's initiative and momentum; Sikh's decisive responsibility; Gurkha's ferocity followed by attention to another person. These are illustrative, not exclusive skills or a mandatory savior beat. No motorcycle/jeep/AK/power reveal yet approved.
4. Selective mission flashes: pursuit, a contested choice, one person trusting another at risk; enough to promise a story rather than a roster. Do not explain all three prior encounters or resolve the missing-informant plot. Pair action with a brief human beat where coherent, so the attraction is to people as well as spectacle.
5. Assembly before the officer: retain the Director's strong team image and make the invitation/mission intelligible. Do not visually establish him as the person who confers all their worth or make the trailer end solely in obedient service. His authority and their independent presence should both read.
6. Closing dramatic hook/title: a brief unresolved complication or protagonist reaction establishes that carrying out a mission will require judgment, not just prowess. Exact line and scene remain unwritten; no unsupported historical quotation. Leave a reason to watch the next release.

This is a series-promise trailer with selected story fragments, not a compressed origin film. Trailer montage chronology is not episode chronology; assembly may come from founding backstory without changing the series opening with the team already working together. Every flash should earn its production cost and ideally support later reusable scene/asset work. Avoid implying footage is from finished episodes when it is purpose-made concept material.

## Archival research lead — verified text, not cleared production asset

Frederick Sleigh Roberts, *Forty-one Years in India*, first published 1897; the inspected Gutenberg transcription reproduces the 1898 first one-volume/29th edition. [Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/16528/16528-h/16528-h.htm), chapter LXVIII, printed pp. 532–534. Text directly inspected on 2026-09-17, not just a search snippet. Roberts ranks communities by supposed military capacity, praises selected groups, and argues that Indian officers cannot replace British leadership. This is evidence of his imperial/racial outlook, not a neutral finding or a universal description of all British opinion.

One short exact candidate excerpt from p. 533: **“Native officers in this respect can never take the place of British officers.”** The surrounding sentence asserts a racial inability to lead; retain that context in research and ensure any use critiques rather than endorses it. The fictional unsupervised trio provides a possible counterpoint. Source author is not our fictional officer and the quotation is not his dialogue. It is not currently selected for the trailer. No narration performer or voice cloning is chosen.

Do not call this memoir a textbook. Recruiting manuals and historical illustrations may provide other leads, but no matching scan or image has been qualified. Before final use, verify quoted wording against a stable edition/page image, date/edition, surrounding argument, attribution, image authenticity and applicable reuse/credit conditions. A source's age or a Gutenberg transcription is not blanket clearance for every scan or illustration. Maintain a quote/asset register separating real sources, accurate recreations, fictional graphics and original promotional copy. No image generation or archival downloading undertaken here.

## Audience-first, bidirectional reuse — accepted exchange 55

Director explicitly wants an attempt at reuse between episodes 1–2 and the trailer, in either production direction, but does not want early reuse to weaken audience cultivation or the first impression. Reuse is an optimization, not a footage quota or a prerequisite that delays the trailer until episodes are complete. Strong trailer-specific material is permitted as a creative choice, subject to normal budget/production approvals; this is not new spending authority.

During the beat sheet/shot breakdown, classify each shot as **shared footage candidate**, **shared scene/assets with a tailored shot**, or **trailer-specific**. Record prospective episode use, whether the scene is accepted or merely a candidate, spoiler/continuity constraints, adaptation work and incremental cost/time when known. Leave unknowns explicit rather than invent savings. Stable scene/shot/asset IDs and versioned source files should preserve usable cameras, lighting, animation, materials and composition; actual schema implementation belongs to D2, not this Markdown decision alone.

Examples, not locked episode placements: gallop/environment/character assets might support episode travel or pursuit, with a trailer-specific camera/edit; mission fragments might reuse accepted episode shots; archival transitions or a signature introduction may remain trailer-only. Do not invent episode story events to amortize trailer footage, change the accepted opening into a founding-mission pilot, leak a key reveal, or reduce an effective trailer to whatever footage happens to exist. Conversely, do not force episode pacing/character logic to accommodate a promotional hero shot. Preserve one coherent visual/world promise even when editing and framing differ.

Decision order: audience/character/story impact first; feasible cost and production constraints assessed honestly; pursue the strongest compatible reuse. Compare options rather than assume maximum reuse is cheapest or best. No numerical reuse target selected. Trailer-specific polish must not imply a recurring quality level the episode workflow cannot sustain without a stated plan.
