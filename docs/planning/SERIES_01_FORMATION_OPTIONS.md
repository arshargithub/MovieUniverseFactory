# Series 01 — prior encounters and formation options

Planning exchange 41. All encounter sketches below are proposals, not accepted events, episode assignments or historical claims.

## Accepted framework

The officer knew the individuals before conceiving the team and then deliberately assembled them. Prior encounters, first joint mission and later earned mutual trust are separate layers. The team has senior secret backing, the officer as sole direct institutional connection, tactical freedom and no British in-field supervision. The series begins with them already together; origins emerge gradually. Exact dates, units, locations, first-meeting sequence and prior links among the trio remain open.

## A. Sikh — the officer backs his judgment, but makes the limit visible

Possible encounter: during a tense effort to secure someone's cooperation, the Sikh gives a personal assurance of protection that helps avert violence. He has judged the immediate situation well but promised beyond what he can independently guarantee. When the intended handling of that person threatens the assurance, he insists the promise matters. The officer listens, obtains a way to keep the person safe while allegations are investigated, and accepts responsibility for the exception. He privately makes clear that the Sikh must not make commitments beyond his authority again.

What each sees: the officer sees initiative and willingness to bear responsibility, not just disobedience; the Sikh experiences genuine respect and effective support, yet learns that his word currently needs the officer's power behind it. This is not absolution for the protected person or a deliberately manufactured debt. No rank, specific legal procedure, culprit or crime is selected.

Possible reason to join later: a chance to exercise the judgment the officer already recognized, with greater practical discretion. Seed of future tension: is that authority his, or merely lent to him?

## B. Gurkha — a difficult disagreement establishes professional respect

Possible encounter: a withdrawal or evacuation leaves an injured local guide outside the planned movement. The Gurkha insists on making a recovery attempt. The officer's concern about exposing others is real; the Gurkha must offer more than courage and cannot assume unlimited support. They work out a bounded attempt; it succeeds at a cost such as delay or lost equipment rather than through effortless heroics. Exact danger, injuries and outcome remain proposals; do not infer a formal period rescue doctrine or suicidal obligation.

What each sees: the officer sees ferocity paired with responsibility to someone the operation no longer needs; the Gurkha sees an officer willing to revise a decision without pretending the risk disappeared. Their trust need not begin with the Gurkha saving the officer's life. Proposed relationship emphasis is professional disagreement survived, not perpetual rescue specialization or infallible Gurkha wisdom.

Possible reason to join later: demanding work that uses his abilities, a consequential place in a small group, and a leader with whom disagreement has proved possible. Terms and compensation must eventually support his material life, but no wage, pension or funded retirement is established.

## C. Pashtun — she enters with her own objective, not as an applicant

Possible encounter: horses passing through her household's trade become implicated in an accusation or disputed transaction. A convenient account would shift responsibility onto her household and other small dealers. She seeks to establish what happened and protect their interests, bringing concrete knowledge of the animals and transaction. The officer initially has an incomplete account. She changes his understanding and negotiates an outcome; when an offer helps only her household, she keeps pressing about the others. Neither acceptance nor refusal of that offer is fixed, since her moral dilemma is provisional.

What each sees: he notices expertise, nerve and independent judgment; she sees that access to him can change a consequential decision but does not mistake him for someone without institutional interests. He does not create her skills or rescue an otherwise passive family. The officer must learn about her fighting capability before team formation, but this meeting need not cram in a fight; source and occasion of that knowledge remain open. No institutional seizure policy, theft, false accuser, real unit or family military contract is selected.

Possible reason to join later: sustained access and influence over decisions affecting her world, as well as a challenge she wants for herself. She brings terms and her own aims; she is not recruited as repayment for a favor or because she has nowhere else to go.

## Comparison and checks

Proposed relationship distinctions: Sikh — being trusted and backed; Gurkha — disagreement and professional respect; Pashtun — negotiation between people with distinct interests. They need not trust the officer equally or interpret his offer identically. These are starting emphases, not exclusive personality roles.

Do not make every meeting an audition, a display of all accepted traits or proof the officer is uniquely benevolent. The sketches explain why each might consider a later invitation, not complete the reasons for long-term trust. Each encounter needs the protagonist's own stakes. His decision to found the team comes after encounters, not as a concealed recruitment test within them.

Next: ask which sketches feel alive, revise/select them, then develop the first joint mission as a test of cooperation. No checklist gate is promoted by this proposal draft.
