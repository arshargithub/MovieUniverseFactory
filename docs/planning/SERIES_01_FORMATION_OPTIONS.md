# Series 01 — prior encounters and formation options

Initially drafted at exchange 41. **Status at exchange 43: all three encounter directions accepted, using the revised Sikh and Pashtun versions below.** Original A/C sketches are retained as superseded alternatives, not current canon. Specific staging, dates, units and episode placement remain open. These are fictional foundations, not historical claims.

## Accepted framework

The officer knew the individuals before conceiving the team and then deliberately assembled them. Prior encounters, first joint mission and later earned mutual trust are separate layers. The team has senior secret backing, the officer as sole direct institutional connection, tactical freedom and no British in-field supervision. The series begins with them already together; origins emerge gradually. Exact dates, units, locations, first-meeting sequence and prior links among the trio remain open.

## A. Sikh — the officer backs his judgment, but makes the limit visible

Possible encounter: during a tense effort to secure someone's cooperation, the Sikh gives a personal assurance of protection that helps avert violence. He has judged the immediate situation well but promised beyond what he can independently guarantee. When the intended handling of that person threatens the assurance, he insists the promise matters. The officer listens, obtains a way to keep the person safe while allegations are investigated, and accepts responsibility for the exception. He privately makes clear that the Sikh must not make commitments beyond his authority again.

What each sees: the officer sees initiative and willingness to bear responsibility, not just disobedience; the Sikh experiences genuine respect and effective support, yet learns that his word currently needs the officer's power behind it. This is not absolution for the protected person or a deliberately manufactured debt. No rank, specific legal procedure, culprit or crime is selected.

Possible reason to join later: a chance to exercise the judgment the officer already recognized, with greater practical discretion. Seed of future tension: is that authority his, or merely lent to him?

## B. Gurkha — a difficult disagreement establishes professional respect

Possible encounter: a withdrawal or evacuation leaves an injured local guide outside the planned movement. The Gurkha insists on making a recovery attempt. The officer's concern about exposing others is real; the Gurkha must offer more than courage and cannot assume unlimited support. They work out a bounded attempt; it succeeds at a cost such as delay or lost equipment rather than through effortless heroics. Exact danger, injuries and outcome remain proposals; do not infer a formal period rescue doctrine or suicidal obligation.

What each sees: the officer sees ferocity paired with responsibility to someone the operation no longer needs; the Gurkha sees an officer willing to revise a decision without pretending the risk disappeared. Their trust need not begin with the Gurkha saving the officer's life. Proposed relationship emphasis is professional disagreement survived, not perpetual rescue specialization or infallible Gurkha wisdom.

Possible reason to join later: demanding work that uses his abilities, a consequential place in a small group, and a leader with whom disagreement has proved possible. Terms and compensation must eventually support his material life, but no wage, pension or funded retirement is established.

## C. Pashtun — she enters with her own objective, not as an applicant

Possible encounter: horses passing through her household's trade become implicated in an accusation or disputed transaction. A convenient account would shift responsibility onto her household and other small dealers. She seeks to establish what happened and protect their interests, bringing concrete knowledge of the animals and transaction. The officer initially has an incomplete account. She changes his understanding and negotiates an outcome; when an offer helps only her household, she keeps pressing about the others. Neither acceptance nor refusal of that offer is fixed, since her moral dilemma is provisional.

What each sees: he notices expertise, nerve and independent judgment; she sees that access to him can change a consequential decision but does not mistake him for someone without institutional interests. He does not create her skills or rescue an otherwise passive family. The officer must learn about her fighting capability before team formation, but this meeting need not cram in a fight; source and occasion of that knowledge remain open. No institutional seizure policy, theft, false accuser, real unit or family military contract is selected.

Possible reason to join later: sustained access and influence over decisions affecting her world, as well as a challenge she wants for herself. She brings terms and her own aims; she is not recruited as repayment for a favor or because she has nowhere else to go.

## Comparison and checks

Proposed relationship distinctions: Sikh — being trusted and backed; Gurkha — disagreement and professional respect; Pashtun — negotiation between people with distinct interests. They need not trust the officer equally or interpret his offer identically. These are starting emphases, not exclusive personality roles.

Do not make every meeting an audition, a display of all accepted traits or proof the officer is uniquely benevolent. The sketches explain why each might consider a later invitation, not complete the reasons for long-term trust. Each encounter needs the protagonist's own stakes. His decision to found the team comes after encounters, not as a concealed recruitment test within them.

Next: ask which sketches feel alive, revise/select them, then develop the first joint mission as a test of cooperation. No checklist gate is promoted by this proposal draft.

## Revision and acceptance — exchanges 42–43 (current direction)

Exchange 42: Director endorses the Gurkha event but challenges the original Sikh/Pashtun encounters because they do not demonstrate capability relevant to the group's dangerous independent missions. Assistant agrees: personal judgment or bargaining alone is an insufficient recruitment basis. This is a substantive correction, not merely additional color. Exchange 43: Director accepts all three after the revisions.

### Sikh — independent command when the plan fails

During an escort or recovery operation, changed circumstances require him to assume responsibility without consulting a superior. He gets people through danger by adapting and making a consequential decision. The accepted sketch includes securing necessary cooperation through a personal assurance beyond his independently guaranteed authority; success leaves him answerable for that promise. The officer recognizes both the operational value of his initiative and the obligation it creates. Exact mission type, protected person, allegation, command status, resolution and dialogue remain open. Recruitment logic: demonstrated capacity to lead and decide when instructions no longer suffice. Do not automatically import every detail of the original A sketch, such as a particular investigation procedure or reprimand.

### Gurkha — recovery under competing responsibilities

Retain the withdrawal/evacuation and injured-guide recovery direction from B. He insists on an attempt; the officer has a legitimate concern for others' safety. They arrive at a limited attempt that succeeds with a cost rather than effortless heroism. Particular terrain, enemy, injuries, equipment losses, timing and exact execution are not locked. Recruitment logic: courage, responsibility and judgment under pressure, plus a professional disagreement that establishes respect.

### Pashtun — overlapping objectives in a dangerous recovery

She is independently trying to recover horses taken from the family's care; the officer is investigating the same group for a different reason. Her knowledge of the animals, transaction and people has already enabled progress. Their efforts intersect and she actively participates in recovery, demonstrating riding, fighting and adaptation under danger, while her own objective can conflict with his preferred outcome. A person taken with the horses remains optional, not an accepted kidnapping/family tragedy. Exact adversary, offense, route, tactics and outcome remain open. This replaces the primarily commercial-dispute encounter in C and supplies the intended pre-team encounter with real danger. Recruitment logic: independent initiative, specific knowledge and effective action, not just persuasive argument. The officer does not create her competence.

### Accepted common basis and remaining scope

The officer has operationally relevant evidence of each person's capability, not merely an impression of admirable character. Sikh: responsibility beyond authority; Gurkha: challenging a decision responsibly; Pashtun: pursuing an independent objective. These are not fixed combat specialties or guarantees of flawless performance. He need not personally witness every moment for the evidence to be credible; exact observation/reporting remains a staging question. No particular meeting order or first joint mission is selected, and prior relationships among the trio remain open. These accepted directions advance the still-open history/relationships work; they do not by themselves complete A3/A4 or assign episode plots.

## First joint mission — next proposal, not accepted

Recommendation: test cooperation rather than stage a second set of auditions. Illustrative premise: locate and bring back a missing intermediary before their disappearance triggers a wider confrontation. The intermediary has reasons to distrust returning. This can expose different definitions of success: the Sikh seeks a promise that will secure cooperation, the Gurkha weighs what happens to someone unable to travel safely, and the Pashtun questions whose interests the return serves. These are possible pressures, not mandatory beats; avoid mechanically displaying every trait or repeating the prior encounters. The officer's briefing is not presumed a lie, the intermediary is not presumed innocent, and the trio's eventual decision is open. Select or replace the premise before expanding it. First joint mission remains backstory, not automatically the pilot.

## First joint mission premise accepted — exchange 44

Director likes the missing-intermediary recovery premise and correctly challenges the previous response's artificial contrast with an explicitly military objective. For this fictional story, a recovery can directly serve military aims without being a battlefield assault. Accepted direction: locate/recover the missing intermediary, with potential wider confrontation as stakes. Whether the intermediary is being held unwillingly, staying willingly, or subject to mixed pressures remains open. Do not interpret “willingly or unwillingly being kept” as authorization or a settled order to bring someone back by force. Their willingness to remain where they are and their willingness to return with the team are separate questions. The earlier assertion that they have reasons to distrust returning is not a settled psychological fact.

Next development requirements: specify why the intermediary matters, why recovery is urgent, and why public British involvement is unacceptable. Possible explanation for discussion, not canon: their role in a sensitive British-backed negotiation cannot be acknowledged; a public search could expose that relationship and undermine the intended arrangement. Specific intelligence, parties, political purpose, detention circumstances, orders, consent, deadline and outcome remain open. No kidnapping, assassination plot, deliberate deception by the officer or specific historical incident is implied. The mission remains the first joint mission in backstory, not a replacement for the accepted opening with the team already together. No release gate closes merely from accepting the premise.
