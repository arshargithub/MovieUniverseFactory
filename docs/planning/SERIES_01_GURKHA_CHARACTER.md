# Series 01 — Gurkha protagonist development

Established at planning exchange 10. This is a developing character brief, not a completed biography or historical qualification. See [series discovery](SERIES_01_DISCOVERY.md).

## Accepted foundation

- A Hindu Gurkha protagonist, one of three leads working with the British officer (previous Director premise).
- Older than the Sikh protagonist, but not by much. No numerical ages or age gap selected.
- Accepting in temperament; what he accepts and where acceptance ends require further development.
- In some ways the person who keeps the three together. This does not automatically make him their formal leader, mediator in every dispute or an infallible mentor.
- A ferocious fighter. Treat this as this individual's developed ability and conduct, not an inherited ethnic quality. Training and service history remain open.

Source for the new traits: Director, exchange 10: “I see him as older, not by a lot, but older. accepting. in some ways the one that keeps the three together. but a ferocious fighter nonetheless.”

## Interpretation to explore — not yet accepted

He may accept people without needing to approve of every choice they make. That could let him hold the group together without always smoothing over disagreement. His ferocity need not be a second personality or a contradiction of warmth; the circumstances and choices that bring it out need development.

Avoid defaulting to the loyal/stoic Gurkha stereotype, assigning spiritual wisdom from religion, or treating him as a caretaker whose only purpose is to support the other leads. He needs his own want, stake, limits and potential mistakes.

Possible underlying tension: knowing how to live with imperfect people and institutions may help him keep the team alive, but could also make him accommodate something he ought to challenge. This is a hypothesis, not accepted resignation, colonial loyalty or approval of injustice.

## Next discovery question

What does “accepting” mean to the Director: primarily generous toward people's imperfections, more reconciled to circumstances he cannot control, or another quality? These can coexist but must not be assumed interchangeable.

## Remaining brief criteria

Identity/temperament foundation established. Personal history and richer relationships, external aim and joining/staying motives, private need/contradiction, moral boundary and consequential episode 1–2 choices remain open. The initial group-cohesion role is a relationship seed, not a completed relationship history. No names, family structure, rank, service history or mission events are inferred.
