## What the test can and cannot establish

- **Attraction:** proposed measures include platform-appropriate exposure-to-watch behavior, qualified views and early retention. Raw views alone do not demonstrate attachment; paid/organic distribution and exposure must be distinguished if used.
- **Engagement/comprehension:** retention/completion alongside understandable stakes, recognizable character differences and emotional interest. Do not require prior historical knowledge or infer comprehension solely from a completed play.
- **Intent to return:** proposed signals include follows/subscriptions, interest in a next release and voluntary requests to see more. These are proxies, not proof of recurring viewership.
- **Actual return:** measure across subsequent releases using available platform analytics; a single trailer cannot qualify durable audience retention. No assumed access to cross-platform personal tracking.
- **Representation:** seek specific feedback on character dimensionality, cultural/religious detail and perceived stereotypes from relevant informed viewers/reviewers. This is distinct from popularity and cannot be reduced to one community-wide approval score.

Before publication, C1/C2/C4 must define platform, audience, duration/format, metric definitions, observation window, exposure context, practical thresholds and what results would prompt revision versus further production. No invented universal benchmark, guaranteed virality or automatic causal conclusion from a small test. Insufficient exposure is inconclusive, not proof the concept failed.

## Exchange 59 — distribution challenge and revised recommendation

Director challenges restricting complete episodes to widescreen/approximately five minutes/YouTube because it may unnecessarily limit audience. This objection is recorded; exchange-58 proposal was never accepted. Platform popularity alone does not quantify reachable audience or establish an optimal episode duration. Nonetheless, requiring a platform switch adds an avoidable step before viewers can enjoy the complete story.

Revised recommendation, awaiting acceptance: publish complete initial installments natively on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, with 9:16 as the primary initial composition. Start outlining approximately 90–180-second episodes (provisional, not a performance benchmark), each offering a dramatic payoff plus continuity/hook. Do not reduce an episode to an advertisement for another platform or arbitrarily split a five-minute film. Keep trailer around the working 60–75-second envelope unless its animatic suggests otherwise. Widescreen versions/compilations remain optional later deliverables, justified by audience evidence and incremental production cost; do not automatically double launch outputs.

Cinematic quality concerns staging, lighting, performance, editing and sound, not exclusively aspect ratio. Vertical requires deliberate approaches to gallop scale and ensemble blocking; it is not a free crop. Shorter episodes require tighter dramatic scope and staged character revelation, not necessarily faster cutting or shallow characters. Before format approval, test whether the episode outlines carry satisfying choices in the proposed duration. Cross-posting creates publishing/analytics/rights work and does not guarantee distribution or three times the audience. Measure retention, next-installment interest and actual return separately by platform; raw view definitions are not interchangeable.
