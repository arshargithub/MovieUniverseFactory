## Strategic reassessment: wrapped support is not loose drape

Initial local tests used one material-space strip with a supported crown and loose face span. They failed: an incorrectly active rest key first replaced the dressed shape; an unevaluated donor transform displaced the body; subsequent free spans slipped below/around the face, with additional modifier/contact sensitivity. Preserved controls distinguish self-contact, rest shape, external contact and removal of the topology modifier. They do **not** isolate a universal Blender solver defect. Earlier crown sizing and face winding also needed correction. A clean build or finite solver was never promoted to a visual pass.

Chosen route: author the tightly wrapped/tucked state, transfer support during the hand's pull, and simulate the loosened span. This is a declared hybrid, not simulation of tying an entire turban. The crown and free end remain one indexed cloth mesh. The two ends of the face span and their support ownership are explicit. The lowered end currently uses a proposed chest tuck; that is not evidence of an unconstrained free tail or finished relaunch dynamics.

The first bust result did not automatically qualify integration: the tunic's contact envelope differed, and the hand path had to share the cloth endpoint's coordinate space. `cloth_release-03` adds the tunic clearance envelope and a farther-forward pull. Geometry tests check outward face winding and reject unreachable two-bone controls rather than stretching limbs. Neither test establishes cultural authenticity, textile parameters or final appearance.

### Concrete carry-forward from this pass

1. A wrapped garment's resting support and a freely draping span are different systems. Make support transfer explicit and test it; do not assume one unconstrained simulation can infer a tuck from appearance.
2. A bust collision control is insufficient for a new tunic and posture. Bind the final relevant body/garment envelope early; verify the hand and cloth in the same transform space.
3. Preserve fixed limb lengths and test reachable targets before animation. Passing IK still does not establish natural anatomy or hand silhouette.
4. Review framing changes for both occlusion **and lost story information**. Removing a foreground obstruction can accidentally remove the corridor-opening evidence.
5. Do not promote cheap whole-sequence proxy rendering into final quality, nor repeatedly refine proxies before the identity/representation boundary is settled. Keep the failed native gate visible; request only the genuine creative choice, not technical endorsement.
