## Voice and sound direction

**Officer:** adult British male voice with lived-in authority, conversational restraint and genuine regard. The sword adds restrained leather/metal contact, not constant exaggerated jangling. Sound perspective should follow the camera without making a close face shot sound like a distant long shot.

**Recognition:** briefly thin the music/ambience to create attention, retaining the gallop. Do not add a horror sting or a frightened gasp inconsistent with her composure. **Turn:** emphasize the load, scrape, gravel release and surge; no slow-motion whoosh. **Uphill:** return momentum and effort, then hold enough sonic continuity for the next character/ensemble transition.

**Music:** a short, reusable motif with propulsion, space for voice and a release into determined continuation. Avoid wallpaper scoring, generic “exotic” timbres or sacred chants as identity shorthand. Cultural instrumentation may be used deliberately after research and creative selection; it is not required to prove her ethnicity. Keep music, voice, effects and ambience on separate stems with cue IDs and source/rights records.
