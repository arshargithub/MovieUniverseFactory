# Pashtun entrance narration and sound proposal

Exchange209, 2026-10-11. Proposed creative decision for the existing18-second entrance; no voice, script, music provider or paid generation is selected. Keep the approved action and camera timing. The purpose is to introduce a particular capable woman inside the officer's imperial frame, not deliver a history lecture or claim that colonial racial categories are true.

## Recommendation

Use treatment A below: a brief historical classification at the opening, then the officer's personal trust as she chooses her own route. Keep the recognition, checkpoint view and dramatic turn largely free of speech and quote graphics. The contrast is between the language used to classify people and an individual exercising judgment—not an instant conversion of the officer into a fully disillusioned anti-imperialist.

The broader martial-races theme belongs across the ensemble trailer. This18-second unit can seed it; it cannot responsibly explain the entire doctrine, her background and the mission while also making the escape legible. Do not lengthen or slow the liked turn to fit exposition. If a performed reading cannot fit naturally, shorten the words first.

## Two treatments for selection

All unquoted sentences below are new fictional dialogue, not historical quotations. Exact timing is a scratch-edit hypothesis; a real read must prove it. Do not create a supposedly authentic book page containing our original dialogue.

### A Classification and personal judgment

Opening, approximately0–5seconds, officer voice:

> We call them martial races. The Pathans: “a hardy and martial people.”

Recognition, checkpoint and turn: no further narration. Hooves, breath, leather and displaced gravel carry the choice and its physical cost. Fade the archival text before the over-shoulder geography shot; do not cover the face or the alternative route.

During the final determined continuation, approximately14–17.5seconds:

> I trust her to find another way.

Nineteen spoken words in total. The five-word quotation is a contiguous excerpt, not Churchill's complete sentence. This is deliberately not a claim that he wrote about this woman. The officer's personal line is sincere and compatible with his accepted respect for the team; he still presumes authority to set the mission.

Why recommended: direct connection to the series theme, a readable relationship between voice and action, and sufficient silence for the turn. Risk: the opening classification can sound like the film's endorsement if delivered as a triumphant slogan. Direct it as his inherited professional vocabulary; let her initiative complicate it. Do not add caricatured accents or cultural shorthand.

### B Mistrust and earned trust

Opening, approximately0–4.5seconds:

> Warburton wrote of “this thick crust of mistrust.”

Final continuation, approximately14–17.5seconds:

> Trust isn't inherited. It is earned.

Fourteen spoken words. The five-word quotation is a contiguous excerpt from Warburton's account of his relationship with Afridis. This option foregrounds relationship rather than military classification; the wider trailer would need to establish the martial-races theme elsewhere.

Why it may appeal: quieter, more intimate officer narration with room for sound. Why not the first recommendation: more abstract, and it risks borrowing Warburton's paternalistic self-portrait to validate the officer. This is not evidence that the protagonist is Afridi; no tribal identity has been selected. If chosen, preserve that distinction in research/credits and do not invent it in her biography.

His personal relationships and the imperial violence around them coexist in the passage. ## Voice and sound direction

**Officer:** adult British male voice with lived-in authority, conversational restraint and genuine regard. Clear rather than theatrical; no booming trailer announcer, sneering villain, comic colonial accent or imitation of a named living performer. Exact accent, age and vocal identity remain a Director choice after a small in-context audition. The character's rank/class/commission history is not fixed by choosing a voice.

**Physical sound first:** surface-synchronized hoof impacts, horse breath, saddle/leather movement, soft cloth, air and gravel. The sword adds restrained leather/metal contact, not constant exaggerated jangling. Sound perspective should follow the camera without making a close face shot sound like a distant long shot.

**Recognition:** briefly thin the music/ambience to create attention, retaining the gallop. Do not add a horror sting or a frightened gasp inconsistent with her composure. **Turn:** emphasize the load, scrape, gravel release and surge; no slow-motion whoosh. **Uphill:** return momentum and effort, then hold enough sonic continuity for the next character/ensemble transition.

**Music:** a short, reusable motif with propulsion, space for voice and a release into determined continuation. Avoid wallpaper scoring, generic “exotic” timbres or sacred chants as identity shorthand. Cultural instrumentation may be used deliberately after research and creative selection; it is not required to prove her ethnicity. Keep music, voice, effects and ambience on separate stems with cue IDs and source/rights records.
