## Proposed visual promise

**An illustrated adventure with cinematic weight and intimate human performances.** The world is visibly drawn and painted, but its people experience real effort, affection, fear and consequences. Neither photorealism with a texture filter nor still illustrations moved around in place of acting.

## Proposed design rules

1. **Drawing and form:** believable adult proportions, individual silhouettes and expressive faces. Ink-like contours and designed shadow shapes describe important forms; do not outline every surface equally. Let face, hand and gesture detail carry attention. No default superhero physique or exaggerated ethnic shorthand.
2. **Paint and material:** retain C's broad painted masses and broken, tactile edges. Cloth, leather, stone and steel remain distinguishable through shape, response to light and selective marks, not pore-level texture. Surface texture should belong to the objects rather than crawl independently across frames.
3. **Color and light:** start from indigo, ochre, terracotta, charcoal and warm ivory. This is a palette family, not an unchanging filter. Allow cool nights, green cultivated places, rain and domestic warmth when story calls for them. Design readable light/shadow groups; faces and important gestures must survive phone-size viewing. Do not assign one immutable color to an ethnic/religious identity.
4. **World and framing:** build credible inhabited places, with paths, wear and ordinary life rather than endless spectacular fortresses. Use foreground, middle distance and background for depth. Native vertical shots prioritize readable relationships and action; mountain/architecture height and receding paths provide scale. Heroic framing is earned, not the only camera language.
5. **Performance:** keep bodies physically convincing and expressions subtle enough for doubt and humor. The Sikh must look like the same person when formidable on horseback and goofy with his sister. The Gurkha's calm, older warmth must coexist with his ferocity; the Pashtun's persistence and negotiation must retain fire. Avoid permanently scowling heroes.
6. **Action and emphasis:** favor clear anticipation, decisive bursts and visible recovery. Horses retain weight and coordinated gait; people tire, brace and react. Selected moments may use a held pose, simplified background or brief graphic accent. These should clarify a dramatic beat, not imply powers outside accepted world rules. Consequential violence without graphic gore remains the baseline.

## First decision: motion grammar (open)

Recommended: **cinematic movement with selective illustrated punctuation**. Continuous, readable acting and spatial movement provide the base; an occasional composition or timing accent supplies the graphic-novel emphasis. Do not set frame rate or animation cadence from a still image.

Alternative: overt panel-to-panel storytelling, using more held compositions, cuts between graphic tableaux and deliberately fragmented movement. Potentially appealing as an intentional language, but it changes the viewing experience and must not be assumed to cost less or pass as performance without testing.

Illustrative proposal, not locked choreography: the Pashtun's gallop flows through space, cloth and dust forming designed shapes; one strong silhouette receives a short emphasis, then the motion continues. The Sikh's entrance finds drama in a horse settling, a turn of the head and a decision to advance. The Gurkha's entrance contrasts patient observation, a short readable burst and a return to care. Existing entrance assignments remain unchanged.
