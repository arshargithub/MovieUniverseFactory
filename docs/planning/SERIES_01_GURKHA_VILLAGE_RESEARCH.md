# Gurkha protagonist — village-return possibilities

Quick research pass, planning exchange 14, 2026-09-17. Purpose: offer grounded fictional possibilities for the Director's accepted village-retirement dream. **No ethnicity, village, household, occupation or new trait is selected by this research.** This is not completed historical validation of an 1870s–1880s setting.

## Findings and limits

| Source inspected | Useful evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| [National Army Museum, The Gurkhas](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/gurkhas) | Gurkhas encompass multiple ethnic groups, clans and tribes. | Military overview, not a nineteenth-century village ethnography. Gurkha does not automatically mean Gurung. |
| [Francis Buchanan Hamilton, An Account of the Kingdom of Nepal (1819)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/30364/30364-h/30364-h.htm), chapter I printed pp. 25–27 and chapter II pp. 73–74 | Describes Gurung seasonal sheep pastoralism and male blanket weaving; describes irrigated terraces and other hill cultivation. | Earlier than our candidate setting and often based on informants. A colonial source containing overtly prejudiced ethnic judgments; those judgments are not evidence of character or inherited traits. Agricultural passages are region-specific, not proof every community used every crop. |
| [Nepal NFDIN, Magar profile](https://nfdin.gov.np/pages/magar/) (Nepali-language institutional profile) | Describes diverse Magar communities, farming and animal husbandry, and Hindu identification alongside indigenous religious traditions. | Modern profile, not direct evidence for a specific village in 1878. Its contemporary census proportions and institutions must not be projected backward. |
| [Nepal NFDIN, Gurung/Tamu profile](https://nfdin.gov.np/pages/gurung/) (Nepali-language institutional profile) | Describes a history involving pastoralism, cultivation and military employment, with distinct religious traditions and Hindu influences. | Modern synthesis. Cannot assign a generic Hindu ritual life to a Gurung household or backdate present practices without local evidence. |

Confidence: cultivation/livestock is a defensible **broad historical direction**, not a fully verified personal livelihood. Sheep-and-wool is a more community-specific lead with explicit early-nineteenth-century support. Neither establishes this soldier's ability to buy land or a pension entitlement. The research does not show that all veterans returned home or that village life was easy.

## Fictional options grounded in those findings

1. **Recommended: a modest cultivating household with a few animals.** He imagines tending land, looking after animals and being present through ordinary village seasons. Exact crops, animals, land access and household must follow community/location research. His wish can be for useful, skilled work whose results remain at home. This fits his accepted need for purpose without requiring that warrior life has become hateful to him. A smallholding is a candidate aspiration, not established ownership or a guaranteed affordable retirement.
2. **Conditional alternative: sheep and wool work.** If a Gurung background becomes the choice, pastoral work and blanket-making are promising period-grounded details. They could preserve physical activity and craftsmanship outside warfare. Seasonal herding may entail substantial travel and hard labour, so it is not automatically restful retirement. Do not transfer this specificity to an unspecified Magar/Chhetri household or use it to decide his ethnicity by convenience.
3. **Social layer, not a verified historical job: a familiar, useful neighbour.** The Director's people-accepting character could imagine helping with everyday work, conversation and disagreements, without aspiring to command. This is our fictional interpretation of his temperament, not proof of a customary veteran role. Do not automatically appoint him village headman, religious elder or a trainer of recruits.

Suggested emotional image, not canon: a morning tending his own patch of land or animals, and an evening among people who know him outside military service. He can dream of usefulness without needing to be exceptional. The possibility that ordinary usefulness might feel insufficient remains a question—not an approved inner conflict.

## Community and religion decision

The accepted Hindu identity does not yet identify his ethnic community. Magar is worth investigating alongside other recruitment backgrounds, but modern religious statistics are not enough to settle a nineteenth-century character. Before freezing rituals, kinship, village architecture, dress or livelihood, choose community and locality, then corroborate with period-specific and Nepali scholarship. Keep Hindu identification compatible with locally specific practice rather than treating it as a uniform template.

## Not yet supported / avoid by default

- Modern pension-funded houses, tourist lodges, tea-shop retirement, schoolmaster roles or formal martial-arts schools as supposedly typical period outcomes.
- A wife, children, bereavement, landed status, village office or a dying-before-retirement arc inferred from the dream.
- The assumption that peaceful village work lacks hardship, obligation or purpose.
- A particular recruitment/service/discharge pathway or precise funding for land. These depend on date, unit, rank and his unofficial arrangement with the officer.
