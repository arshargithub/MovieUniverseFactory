# Series 01 — visual direction, genre and world rules

Established at exchange 51, 2026-09-17. Decision brief, not an approved style or replacement universe. This raises a foundational choice before detailed plot/script and asset lock. It does not discard existing characters, themes or technical evidence.

## Director inputs and status

- Director asks for explicit decisions on how the film looks, citing a comic book come alive, hyperrealistic 3D, Fantastic Mr. Fox-like presentation and retro possibilities. These are alternatives, not a selected look, animation technique or artist imitation request.
- Director explores mixed-era signature imagery: Pashtun on a horse, Sikh on an old British motorcycle, Gurkha in a military-jeep-style off-roader; possible AK-47 for the Pashtun, kirpan/sword for the Sikh and kukri for the Gurkha. Preserve as exploratory ideas, not locked loadouts, ownership, religious practices or exclusive equipment. Anachronistic machinery is a world decision, not just a rendering choice.
- Director explores one distinctive ability per protagonist near the boundary of human possibility and superhero/divine capability. No ability, cause, hereditary mechanism, spiritual doctrine or power scale selected.
- Prior working frame was nineteenth-century-inspired historical fiction. Director already accepted fiction rather than documentary, but that alone did not authorize a mixed-era or supernatural world. Keep the prior frame as a baseline while choosing deliberately. Real cultural identities, colonial tensions, relationships and motivations remain valuable under either branch.

## Four separate decisions

1. **Visual language / art direction:** character shape/proportion, silhouettes, materials, palette, lighting, environments, costume and prop design, composition/camera, animation timing and treatment of violence. Outputs: approved visual-direction brief/look bible and representative comparisons. Medium/workflow (3D, drawn, hybrid) is a separate implementation choice; Blender does not require photorealism.
2. **Genre and tone:** audience promise—e.g., action-adventure/espionage with historical political drama, or an explicitly speculative adventure. Decide humor, danger, emotional realism and violence intensity. Historical fiction is not the only possible story engine; a historical setting can coexist with several genres.
3. **World model / technology rules:** our historical timeline, a counterfactual timeline, or an openly reimagined history-inspired world with deliberate mixed-era technology. Decide what history stays real and what changes. Define availability and limits for weapons, transport, communications, medicine, manufacture/fuel/ammunition and who has access. Avoid importing phones or later political history automatically because one later weapon appears. If a named AK-47 rather than an invented analogue is retained, its Earth-history associations and origins need a deliberate policy.
4. **Heightening / extraordinary-ability rules:** exceptional but human skill; explicitly ambiguous near-superhuman feats; or objectively supernatural abilities. Decide knowledge, prevalence, origin, training, limits, costs, failure, recovery, counters and social consequences. Audience uncertainty may be intentional; writers still need boundaries. Do not replace grounded competence and moral choices with powers that remove danger or solve every information gap.

## Terminology and source limits

These labels guide discussion, not rigid boxes. Alternate history ordinarily involves a changed history with an explicit or implicit divergence; see [The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction, Alternate History](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/alternate_history) and [Jonbar Point](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/jonbar_point). Search-indexed text was inspected; direct alternate-history page retrieval failed. A freely mixed-era world need not pretend to offer a rigorously derived counterfactual timeline.

Magical realism is not a generic synonym for special abilities. The reference description emphasizes extraordinary events integrated into realistic narrative without treating their improbability as a problem: [Encyclopedia.com, Magic Realism](https://www.encyclopedia.com/literature-and-arts/literature-other-modern-languages/latin-american-literature/magic-realism) (indexed reference text inspected). For individually defined combat abilities, “heightened action” or “low-level supernatural abilities” is clearer production shorthand until the treatment is chosen. “History-inspired speculative action-adventure” is a proposed descriptive label, not an asserted formal genre category. Retrofuturist/steampunk labels are optional and not automatically appropriate merely because objects from different eras coexist.

## Recommendations for discussion — none selected

- Explore a **history-inspired speculative action-adventure in a deliberately reimagined frontier world**, preserving emotional/cultural specificity while openly allowing chosen technological and fantastic departures. Compare this with a grounded historical branch before selecting; no exact real year with unexplained later technology by default.
- Leading visual candidate: **stylized cinematic 3D with a graphic-novel sensibility**—designed silhouettes, selective painted/textured surfaces, expressive faces and purposeful lighting, physical weight with controlled exaggeration. This is a taste recommendation, not a promise of low cost or proof the current harness can deliver it. Retain photoreal and tactile/stop-motion-inspired options for comparison.
- Define powers conservatively before naming them: one bounded capability per person, with visible cost or limits, if Director selects that path. Never make actual ethnic/religious membership evidence of innate superior fighting ability; that would risk validating the racial theory the series examines. Do not infer gods or powers from the three religions. Individual experience, acquired skill or another deliberately chosen explanation can preserve cultural richness without treating community as a biological power class.
- Preserve broad dramatic decisions while revalidating mechanics: improved firearms change sword encounters; engines change travel/time limits; communications change isolation; enhanced perception or recovery changes the informant mission. Horse use needs a role within the world's terrain/logistics/style conventions, not arbitrary inferiority. Initial Blender qualification remains engineering evidence, not validation of a new style or effects system.

## Concrete acceptance outputs and order

1. **A2 world/genre brief:** choose branch, genre/tone, relationship to real history, technology envelope, ability level and rule boundaries; identify research and representation questions. No need for an encyclopedia before scenes, but freeze rules on which a scene depends.
2. **A5 visual-direction brief/look bible:** explicit design rules, reference attributes (what to borrow and avoid), character/environment/prop coherence and representative same-content style comparisons. Compare the same character/environment/action rather than unrelated attractive images. After a direction is shortlisted, use a short motion sample to test appeal and readability before production-scale asset lock; coordinate with C2 output framing and E1, reusing material rather than starting a generic campaign per style.
3. **Impact review:** map each accepted story/character/mechanic to KEEP, ADAPT or OPEN, with Director approval of substantive changes. Preserve prior versions and reasons. Reconcile applicable legacy art/asset/UX decisions under D1 instead of claiming this brief supersedes the entire source corpus.
4. **E1/E4 production evidence:** prove the chosen look, motion, revision, identity consistency and time/cost on representative series material. Still-image appeal alone does not pass motion or end-to-end production gates.
