## Appearance failures

These are visible at intended framing, not demands for hero-level microscopic detail.

**Face, beard and turban:** the face is sharply simplified, with a narrow, pointed, highly regular beard mass. Fine fibers do not rescue that mass. The tall smooth crown and sparse wrap cues read as an assembled cap rather than convincing layered cloth. His expression and posture provide little of the commanding human presence required for this acknowledgment. He need not resemble the principal Sikh, but he must inhabit the same production as the Pashtun.

**Outfit:** broad smooth chest/shoulder areas look like a fitted shell. The diagonal sash and waist layer have too little visible thickness, tension, bunching or material hierarchy. Trousers follow the limbs like shaped tubes; the added surface variation does not establish cloth construction. Visible folds must follow support and compression, not simply be present somewhere on the mesh. This is a form/drape problem before it is a texture-resolution problem.

**Saddle and tack:** the blue saddle cloth reads as a projecting flat panel with an abrupt triangular edge. Its thickness, drape and compression under seat/thigh are not convincing. The seat, thigh, boot, stirrup and leather need to read as a coherent supporting assembly. The previous test found and removed a rail intersection, but absence of that intersection did not make the saddle believable. A larger saddle-material library would not fix the current form by itself.

**Horse appearance:** dark colour was delivered, but broad smooth highlights and weakly legible coat detail still give a polished sculpture impression. Subdivision rounded the muzzle; it did not establish a convincing eyelid/muzzle/coat hierarchy at this distance. The compressed dark material range and current lighting are plausible contributors, not isolated proven causes. Matched clay and material/light controls should separate shape from surface. Do not brighten or recolour the horse arbitrarily to conceal deformation.

**Crowd carry-forward:** the existing attention constraint and differentiated timing remain useful engineering work. They do not admit clothing, faces or natural acting. Near actors still need a representative same-film appearance proof before propagation. Keeping eyes aimed at the rider is necessary for the scene but not sufficient evidence of purposeful movement.

## What actually passed and why that was insufficient

The prior review recorded attached cuffs/sashes, supported boots/stirrups, changed head/torso/leg poses, maintained hoof descent speed, differentiated crowd steps and gaze,190 inspected frame thumbnails, selected full-size controls and successful reopen tests. These were legitimate partial checks. The six fresh-open controls and protected-object checks need not be retracted as technical results.

However, the final prose described supported cloth/folds and queued same-film appearance for the Director without rejecting the obvious smooth-shell clothing and schematic saddle. There was **no sufficient artistic basis for that recommendation**. The formal knowledge gate was still `ready_for_review:false`;313 permitted a separate manual AV queue, not a waiver for visible failures. Calling the handoff “not a production pass” did not make it appropriate.

The failures are:

1. **Method:**320's `warp_rear` changes existing keyframe times. The inherited horse method combines a global hind-support pivot with prescribed joint curves, fixed hind-foot targets and small chest/hip offsets. This can be a useful rigging mechanism, but it was not shown to produce the reference's coupled poses. Additional rider bell-shaped envelopes were layered around it. Retiming preserved much of the problematic shape trajectory.
2. **Judgment:** “there is relative motion,” “the folds exist,” and “the gap is gone” replaced whole-result judgments. The already-rejected319 appearance became the practical comparison despite naming Pashtun in the document.
3. **Evidence adequacy:** a grid can expose discontinuities and missing poses but can conceal face/cloth/saddle weaknesses. Full-size controls were present; their conclusions were still too generous. This is not solved merely by increasing the image count.
4. **Admission:** human-pending audiovisual judgments were allowed to absorb agent-owned appearance and pose failures. The actual313 rule already forbids this.
5. **Strategy compliance:** one representative actor was supposed to pass before propagation.320 explicitly records that this was not followed. Repeated patches and more versions did not trigger a sufficiently material reconstruction of the failing forms/performance.

Retrieval supplied the relevant animal coordination, look, visual gate and anticipation obligations. Fresh321 retrieval does so again. The earlier271 character-rejection audit describes several of the same failure modes. More rules or context alone are not the demonstrated remedy. There is no evidence here that context compaction caused this rejection, nor that Blender or agent-only production has reached an inherent limit.

## Recommended change in execution and admission

Apply existing rules through concrete artifacts rather than another long general checklist. These are internal work stages, not new routine Director approvals.

1. **Reference pose map before another retime.** Annotate whole horse/rider silhouettes at gather, drive, crest, reach/contact and compression/recovery. Mark hind support, pelvis/chest, poll/muzzle, foreleg folding and rider seat/shoulder/hand relationships together. Preserve the intention without pretending to solve3D motion uniquely from a single edited2D view.
2. **Prove the pose family with the existing rig.** Reauthor the complete supported shapes and breakdowns, not just their timing. A breakdown is the intentional transition pose explaining how one main pose becomes the next. Test whether support constraints prevent the required crouch, hock bend or landing absorption; revise that representation if they do. Do not protect a convenient global-pivot path as if it were accepted art.
3. **Prove appearance separately but early.** One leader, actual saddle/tack and horse in standing, crest and landing poses, at actual shot size and diagnostic crops, beside the accepted co-star. Reject cylindrical clothes, slab-like tack or schematic face/turban before expensive final motion. Reuse qualified techniques/materials where applicable, not Pashtun's identity or automatic performance approval.
4. **Then integrate the short performance.** Author timing, overlap, support changes and delayed recovery as one action; add cloth/groom/earth response only after it reads in the primary forms. Inspect transition interiors and adjacent contacts. Keep the isolated scope and one-second handles; do not render the whole trailer for each test.
5. **Separate verdicts with vetoes.** Appearance/same-film, pose/contact, temporal performance and technical integrity each get a verdict. A technical pass cannot offset an appearance fail. For every previously escaped failure, show the specific reference/candidate comparison and what changed perceptually—not the method name. Record the strongest remaining counterexample before recommending review.
6. **Limit the manual AV queue to its real purpose.** The Director may judge actual temporal feel, sound and creative acceptance under313. Visible appearance, pose and interface failures must be closed first. An unresolved material method/capability boundary must be presented as that decision, not as a candidate that is “almost there.” Do not ask for another AV provider or an extra review merely to evade this responsibility.

This is still a fallible agent review, not an independent art critic or a guarantee of no future misses. The test of improvement is the next result: fewer repeated escaped defects and no frame-visible known failures at handoff, not the number of added documents/tests. Record this as one rejected handoff with distinct defect groups, not one Director input per sentence.
