## What we actually learned

| Area | Evidence / diagnosis | Carry forward |

Projected scarf/hair and shadows contaminated uncovered regions. [Trial](../planning/SERIES_01_FACEBUILDER_TRIAL_01.md) | Inspect inputs/cameras before fitting. Treat reconstruction as a starting representation, not recovery of true hidden anatomy or clean albedo. |
| Facial identity | Director accepted a somewhat wider/squarer jaw. Matched camera-relative lighting helped distinguish material asymmetry from shape. [Bilateral review](../planning/SERIES_01_BILATERAL_FACE_REVIEW.md) | Freeze the liked identity. Diagnose clay, unlit colour and controlled light only when they answer a specific question; do not reshape the face to compensate for shadows or hair framing. |
| Neck/shoulders | Layered local corrections produced rings, pinches and posterior bulges. A horizontal preservation boundary inadvertently protected part of the defect. [Anatomy](../planning/SERIES_01_NECK_ANATOMY_REVIEW.md), [accepted16](../planning/SERIES_01_NECK_ANATOMY_REVIEW_04.md) | Work on the continuous anatomical transition. Use semantic protection masks, inspect back and obliques, retain collarbone/throat definition. Numerical smoothness alone is not anatomical credibility. |
| Skin/look | Repeated procedural infill became repetitive or airbrushed. Cheek-led authored atlas detail and protected face blending worked better. Residual painted illumination remains. [Accepted atlas02](../planning/SERIES_01_BUST_SKIN_REVIEW_05.md) | Preserve the strongest approved material character and extend it outward; do not flatten the good region to match weak infill. Prove representative lighting, not arbitrary-light perfection. |
| Facial performance | Static painted eyes were insufficient. Independent eye surfaces, blink coverage and local mouth/neck corrections enabled accepted restrained acting. [Performance](../planning/SERIES_01_FACE_SURFACE_INTEGRATION_01.md) | Audit anatomy/controls early. Test actual blink occlusion, neutral return and complete short performance. This does not provide teeth/tongue, phonemes or a general facial rig. |
| Hair representation | Painted scalp plus geometric hair competed. Repeated cap, loft, projection and clump refinements failed the integrated look. More strands and randomness could not repair the underlying flow. [Hair contract/history](CHARACTER_ASSET_LEARNINGS.md) | Separate scalp/skin from hair; choose a representation with coherent root distribution, flow and editable controls before polish. Avoid another parameter loop on a structurally unsuitable method. |
| Feasible design | Preserving the existing free groom's natural part/flow worked better than forcing the illustrated crown. Director explicitly authorized that alternative and ultimately accepted it. [Native groom record](../planning/SERIES_01_NATIVE_GROOM_CAPABILITY.md) | Let tool/asset strengths inform a proposed design while preserving character intent. This is agent-executed adaptation of artist-authored input, not proof of de-novo autonomous hair authorship. |
| Small corrections | Front attachment required moving the growth surface and roots coherently, not merely lifting loose strands. Exposed temple roots made stripes; long aligned tips made a spike. | Diagnose the actual mechanism. Preserve the rest, make one scoped edit, review normal-size whole views as well as crops. ## Why the process became inefficient

There were real reconstruction, anatomical, texture and representation problems—not merely excessive taste. But we optimized isolated defects without a sufficiently explicit production target, feasibility test or stopping rule. Early portrait approval quietly acquired more authority than its scope justified. We spent on hidden/full-angle detail before proving its shot value, and some diagnostic/packaging overhead became repetitive.

The Director also explicitly asked for sustained exact-reference work and later made capability discovery the priority over economics (exchanges138–140). Those were legitimate temporary objectives; persistence was authorized. The failure was not following that instruction. It was failing to surface the target/representation mismatch earlier, repeatedly overrating visual readiness, and failing to distinguish research investment from repeatable production cost. The eventual natural-groom pivot was a material design decision that required—and received—Director approval.

Do not retroactively say the original portraits were “only suggestions” throughout: they were approved identity/hair authorities, subsequently amended by accepted native differences and the explicit alternative. For future assets, state that authority by feature before work begins.
