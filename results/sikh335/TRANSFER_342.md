## Integrated method and early proof

1. Keep the qualified neutral native as input. Build a derivative garment using
   its continuous mesh and draped rest coordinates. Do not change actor anatomy,
   head identity, accepted Pashtun or production camera.
2. Apply the measured neutral-to-actor segment mapping to all existing207 leader
   samples, with dense spear-plant samples retained. Lower-arm ownership must
   include all cuff vertices. Keep the original garment hidden as provenance.
3. Retarget sash support to the actual new shirt. Suppress redundant chest
   overlays only where a continuous garment is underneath; do not conceal holes.
   Check collar/head-neck, wrist/cuff, waist/skirt and shoulder/sash as interfaces.
4. Save/reopen. Inspect actual leader stance, reverse and crest before propagating
   to the nearest crowd actor; inspect that actor before crowd-wide reuse.
5. Complete head/eyes/beard, tied-cloth crown, material and saddle review at shot
   framing. These are original obligations, not waived by a successful shirt.
   Combine with the current motion derivative only after appearance gates.

The first production transfer is a discriminating test, not a promise that cached
linear deformation is sufficient. If it creates flattened armpits or severe inner
elbow folds, change its joint weighting/pose construction. Do not patch each frame
or stamp an approval because the neutral version was good.

## Review and limits

Actor and garment must look inhabited in the same film as the Pashtun, with
readable layered cloth, plausible support and no mannequin-like head/eyes.
Surface noise cannot repair silhouette or contact. A front-only pass is invalid.
Known face/crown/tack failures prevent a whole-character pass. Motion still needs
reference-phase comparison and the explicit Director AV queue; no invented
playback assessment. Keep one Blender job active, no new services/spend/egress,
and preserve all source/candidate/failure history within the shared allowance.
