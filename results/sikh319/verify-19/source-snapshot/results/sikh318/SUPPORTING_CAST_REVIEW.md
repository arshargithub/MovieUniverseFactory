## Leader: one supporting actor, close-shot quality

Priority is the rearing close shot, not an arbitrary polygon count or pore budget.

1. **Whole head, turban and beard.** Retain the established older, commanding blue
   Nihang silhouette. Replace the smooth stacked crown with credible cloth-wrap
   crossings, edge thickness, compression and a readable asymmetrical silhouette,
   grounded in the supplied Nihang reference. Give the beard a shaped mass with
   overlapping clumps and a broken outline, rather than a few solid hanging bars.
   Improve brow, eyelids, cheek/jaw and moustache transitions before individual
   hairs. No new protagonist identity design or invented backstory is needed.
2. **Body and costume construction.** The current sleeves/torso read as tubes;
   boots as rounded caps and the sash as a flat strip. Establish shoulder/elbow
   forms, believable fabric thickness, folds caused by the belt/saddle and bent
   joints, and supported folds across the lap. Preserve connected cuffs and the
   mounted pose. Hands must visibly close around spear/reins, not merely touch
   them. Check both sides at rise, crest and landing, not only a standing pose.
3. **Material hierarchy.** Distinguish matte indigo cloth, warm skin, hair,
   worn leather and restrained metal. Place wear at hems, contact and handling
   zones; use large readable tonal variation before tiny random noise. The torch
   should reveal forms without making skin, cloth and horse equally shiny.
4. **Intent and response.** He notices the rider, acknowledges him, plants the
   spear and coordinates with the horse. Show eye/head intent and shoulder/torso
   preparation; allow arms to absorb motion while hips stay saddle-supported.
   Cloth, beard and pennant follow the action with bounded delay. Do not animate
   every part perpetually or add nervous motion to manufacture life.

He is non-recurring, but a close shot still needs a convincing face, hands and
costume. That does not require a principal's full expression library or unseen
wardrobe/body detail. Build for this greeting/rear and the visible reverse angles.

## Crowd: tiers of detail, not ten new principals

The visible group shares near-identical crowns, beard silhouettes, blue vertical
clothing and regular weapon placement. Even with staggered feet, that repetition
reads as a row of tokens. Current stepping remains a motion-review item, not an
accepted crowd-performance source.

| Screen role | Needed investment | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nearest few figures beside the corridor | Distinct build/height, readable turban/beard shapes, articulated hands/weapon holds, garment folds and grounded step-and-close | Cloning one identical face/costume with random colour |
| Middle figures | Related but different silhouettes, restrained indigo values, shield/weapon spacing, attention and individual reaction delay | Ten elaborate facial rigs that are not visible |
| Distant figures | Convincing group composition, overlapping silhouettes and non-synchronous purposeful motion | Uniform sway, synchronized sidesteps or independent random jitter |

Give each person a small action: notice, check the leader, transfer weight, clear
the corridor, settle and follow the departure with attention. Keep weapons,
wrists, shoulders, feet and costume connected. Body motion should cause secondary
response, rather than disconnected moving accessories. Review whole clothed
figures at intended phone size and the nearest interactions at full resolution.
