## What the evidence says

The supplied footage IS useful positive appearance evidence. In the rise/apex
we can see a rounded shoulder mass, a chest-to-proximal-limb transition and a
connected neck/body silhouette. It therefore contradicts “we have no reference”
or “another image is always necessary.” It is also enough to reject the broad
triangular sheet in our339 image. Our agent should have used that evidence more
effectively rather than converting reduced artifacts into an assumed cure.

However, the horse occupies a small portion of the portrait frame. Dark coat,
overlap, tack/rider and one principal viewing side obscure the opposite junction
that fails in136. It does not directly resolve frontal/reverse volume or all
lower-neck/foreleg contours. The current reference coverage is **PARTIAL**, not
qualified sufficient for this micro-appearance task. Cropping magnifies existing
pixels; it does not create the missing view/detail.

The136 frames still show a pinched/shelved junction at333/342 and an elongated
triangular web at339. These are visibly unacceptable independently of whether
one narrow bright line is rein, highlight or skin. No per-pixel ownership claim
is needed to reject the broad form.

The controlled progression distinguishes transmission from target construction:

and136 cache playback controls within0.12micrometres. Yet the actual images
still failed. Those controls reduce the likelihood that mere interpolation or
failure to transfer the requested coordinates explains the plateau. They do
not prove every rig/weight issue irrelevant; they do demonstrate that accurately
reproducing the proposed target was insufficient. Smoothing/relaxation and more
samples were solving the wrong success proxy.
